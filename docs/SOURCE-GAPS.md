# Geographic source extraction and limits

`python scripts/build_route.py` deterministically reads the supplied references and writes `public/data/route.json`, `terrain.bin`, `tiles/*.json` and `data-validation.json`. It needs Python 3, NumPy, SciPy and Pillow. All reference files remain unchanged.

The selected route comes from the green **driving-track** curve in `references/atlas/maps/dorridge-kidderminster-overview.svg`, reversed into Kidderminster → Dorridge chainage order and resampled every eight metres. It is a previously fitted, source-derived track curve, not a line joining station pins, a surveyed alignment or the midpoint between two running tracks. That source line was authored in the Dorridge → Kidderminster travel direction. Its physical centre remains offset zero. `route.opposingOffsets` recovers a varying left-side counterpart from the mapped adjacent rails, rather than assuming a uniform +4.2 m line on the other side of real platforms. Typical UK direction assignment is an authoring assumption until checked against date-specific operating records.

`python scripts/build_route.py --opposing` regenerates only that counterpart field/metadata; the normal build also regenerates it. It projects actual mapped rail segments onto source-route normal planes, selects connected near-parallel source ways with dynamic programming, excludes mainline-parallel platform polygons, keeps all offsets negative and applies a minimum 3.3 m model centre separation. The final path has 5,917 offsets aligned to unchanged source points/chainages, with 5,680 available mapped candidate samples and 237 inferred samples across 16 explicit inference/gap ranges. The four two-track bores use an explicit -3.6 m counterpart: sparse exported adjacent splines fan implausibly away from the selected curve, especially within Old Hill, and cannot be treated as a verified second bore track. Other missing geometry occurs near Blakedown and at a few source-way transitions. `mappedOffsets`, null gaps, raw pre-inference candidate offsets and source-way IDs are retained separately. A mapped candidate's existence does not imply that its final physically corrected position is an exact source/survey position.

The counterpart includes explicit corrections for source-fit separation, smooth transitions and 1.55 m platform-edge clearance where a nearby feasible path exists. It never changes side through offset zero or jumps beyond a whole platform to hide a conflict. Larger source-vs-model corrections near the multi-track Small Heath/Tyseley approach remain authoring inferences and require operational/geographic review. A few raw platform/fitted-track corridors are geometrically incompatible with both the fixed primary curve and the 3.3 m model separation: source0 has 87 sampled edge-clearance deficits and the counterpart has 46, concentrated at Stourbridge Junction, Galton Bridge and Moor Street. `route.opposingTrack.platformSweptClearanceConflicts` records lane, chainage, platform ID, actual clearance and required edge-adjustment deficit. These source compatibility findings require transparent derived platform-edge/coping authoring or source review; they must not be reported as a raw-source swept-clearance pass. Original reference polygons remain unchanged. Runtime derived geometry requires its own actual volume checks.

The current runtime now constructs a separate, derived platform overlay: it subtracts the model's two swept running envelopes, preserves the original source polygons, rebuilds the remaining boundary walls and anchors furniture to the resulting polygons. This is an explicit model-compatibility correction, not new survey evidence. `scripts/audit-geometry.ts` measures that derived geometry and the actually emitted terrain/asset meshes; `docs/reports/geometry.json` records finite sampling coverage and any remaining contact/clearance/seam failures. Raw source compatibility deficits are therefore not, on their own, proof that the cooked overlay still intersects the train. Nor does clipping alone certify every real or simulated clearance.

The source curve is about 47.328 km long, begins about 320 m before the Kidderminster station control and terminates at the Dorridge control. A reversing/departing consist must remain physically valid at that endpoint; the fitted curve alone does not provide a long operating buffer beyond Dorridge.

The runtime adds approximately 173–180 m of context on each mapped endpoint running path, following connected source rail segments rather than extending an arbitrary straight line. The initial fit and continued vertical grade are explicitly inferred. Physical consist poses outside the playable curve use those same context paths; the playable route limits and source chainages are unchanged. These short buffers do not establish an operating authority or a fully modelled route beyond either endpoint.

The export preserves 22 ordered station controls, 3,217 mapped adjacent-rail segments, 51 source platform polygons, 48,156 building footprints, 19,499 road subpaths and 5,548 land-use polygons in 121 one-kilometre spatial tiles. Each tile's geometry uses local metric coordinates with X east, Y ODN up and Z south. Buildings and land polygons use `[x,z]` footprints; road and rail points use `[x,y,z]`.

Road paths have a single centroid-based source-tile owner. `python scripts/build_route.py --roads` also deterministically exports `public/data/road-surfaces.json`, retaining every path's source ID, coordinates and grade relationships and adding spatial bounds plus source-tile hashes. This shared support dataset lets neighboring terrain builders evaluate the same crossing/approach profile at tile boundaries; it introduces no additional mapped roads or surveyed elevations. Rendering and traffic ownership remain separate from shared terrain support.

`python scripts/build_route.py --land` similarly exports the 129 existing water polygons and their inferred levels into `public/data/land-surfaces.json`, so terrain support does not change across a source-tile ownership boundary. Water levels are source-ground-based estimates, not a hydrological survey. Multipart buildings and water polygons sharing one source relation receive deterministic `/part/<footprint SHA256>` instance suffixes while preserving the original `sourceId`; `--building-ids` and `--water-ids` apply those rules to existing cached tiles without changing geometry. Every multipart water part keeps its own original inferred level, rather than collapsing different river/canal parts through a shared relation ID.

The detailed Snow Hill–Dorridge sheet retains 756 source graph node identities and 827 edges. These IDs establish the supplied source connectivity: crossing in plan does not automatically connect two edges. There are 82 source branch nodes within 18 m of the fitted driving curve, exported as turnout authoring anchors. These are not a complete surveyed switch/interlocking table, and nearby parallel tracks must not be merged merely because they are close. Equivalent western operational node identities are absent from the overview, although adjacent track geometry is available there.

The supplied civil sheet provides 43 rail-bridge, 88 road-bridge and 93 footbridge model footprints in the eastern section, with source-way IDs, inferred structural dimensions and support positions. Their deck levels and supports were already model estimates in the reference. The pipeline exports them as estimates; they are not independently verified structural records. General road/rail bridge, tunnel and layer tags were not retained in most overview SVG titles. Do not infer a bridge at every road–rail plan intersection. The runtime must apply explicit structure relationships and validate its engineering constraints separately.

Bare-earth terrain comes from the supplied Environment Agency 2022 10 m DTM export. The runtime grid samples it at 20 m, at original pixel centres, retaining the original centimetre encoding: `26 + (value - 1) * 0.01`, with zero reserved for nodata. It is a numeric mesh input, not a scene texture. Runtime metadata declares the pixel-centre origin and positive-south row direction. Numerical precision is not survey accuracy.

Rail elevation is an explicit engineering inference from the source ground with smoothing and a 3% profile envelope, not a measured railhead trace. Hard straight-bore profiles are applied to Old Hill and the Hockley tunnels, with a terrain-cover constraint and a lowering-only approach envelope; they do not follow the hilltop terrain inside the bore. The Snow Hill segment recovers the reference chart's existing **estimated** rail profile, including its inferred absolute levels and grade break; no uniform 1:47 bore was imposed. Its source DTM/rail profile may still require engineered cover around portal/cutting transitions, recorded as `minimumModelCover` rather than hidden as a survey pass. Old Hill and the two Hockley tunnel portal intervals are authoring estimates and remain unverified. The approximate Old Hill historical length follows the [Stourbridge Line User Group account](https://www.stourbridgelineusergroup.info/files/Platform-2---Issue-10--A5-Print-Format-.pdf), which gives 896 yards; the supplied video caption gives 895 yd and a [RailwayData register](https://www.railwaydata.co.uk/tunnels/) uses a different endpoint/length convention. Neither provides the missing independently checked modern portals in this export. Geographical placement and current exact spans remain acceptance gaps.

Platform heights are generated relative to the associated route profile, and building heights/styles are the original source's stated style estimates. Default station lengths/platform counts are an explicitly provisional authoring baseline. Source station pins differ from the fitted track by up to about 14.5 m; that is recorded in `referenceOffset` and cannot be represented as a 2 m real-world survey pass.

Blakedown, Cradley Heath, Langley Green and Bentley Heath crossings have reference-correlated authoring anchors. The list does not establish a complete current crossing register, exact barrier type, road position, approach detection or operating order. Precise signal positions, aspect capabilities, speed limits, block boundaries, overlaps, route directions and interlocking tables were not supplied. Any runtime representative devices or schedules must remain labelled inferred/simulator-authored.

Two bounded attempts to supplement the full-route missing structures through Overpass failed: the main endpoint returned HTTP 502 and the alternative timed out. Subsequent direct OpenStreetMap API map requests succeeded for four bounded adjacent-service areas: Stourbridge, The Hawthorns, Smethwick Galton Bridge and Kidderminster. `public/data/adjacent-source.json` preserves the selected ways, source node identities, geographic/projected coordinates, tags, object version/timestamps and response hashes; it is an explicit additional source, not a silent replacement of the uploaded references. Date-specific 2026 equivalence and exact rail elevations still require review.

`python scripts/build_route.py --adjacent` regenerates `public/data/adjacent-services.json` from that cached source plus the main route/terrain, without network access. It exports a connected Class 139 branch path to the supplied Stourbridge Town control, two actual mapped Metro tracks around The Hawthorns, an electrified Stour Valley crossing-line path under Galton Bridge, and static heritage stock on an operator-tagged Severn Valley Railway track at Kidderminster. The Galton lower level is a 7 m clearance inference; runtime terrain, deck and support geometry must respect that independent lower path. Service cycles are representative authoring values, not working timetable records. The Metro geometry and most of the Town branch were absent from the supplied heavy-rail SVG layer; placing those vehicles on the nearest exported heavy-rail line would be incorrect. Separate source node networks remain separate from mainline movement authority.

`public/data/building-access-evidence.json` caches a further bounded diagnostic API check for four complex building/access overlaps, including way versions, timestamps, tags and response hashes. Touchwood Shopping Mall has explicitly tagged roof delivery roads (`layer=1`) and tunnelled arcades (`layer=-1`); Meridian House/Ringway Centre is above Hurst Street's tunnel and a building-passage cycleway; 36 Paradise Street has a covered private driveway; Acra/St Philips Point has covered parking access, a 3.5 m vehicle-height limit and two underground building levels. All four supplied SVG outlines have one subpath, and the queried building ways have no parent relation providing missing courtyard rings. These facts establish real stacked access, but layer tags are not surveyed heights and the current single ground-derived road profiles do not define safe building openings. Such unresolved source instances must retain explicit identity, footprint, position, provenance and rejection reasons; a solid foundation must not silently block their mapped access routes. Their detailed reconstruction remains an infrastructure-fidelity gap.

The paired [GeoJSON evidence](../public/data/building-access-evidence.geojson) preserves the four current source polygons in longitude/latitude, original IDs/versions, exact response hashes, uploaded-model footprints/positions and the rejection rule. The [tag/relationship cache](../public/data/building-access-evidence.json) includes the supporting roads. The asset-fidelity gate for these four instances is **unaccepted** until their levelled access and structural voids can be reconciled; keeping their identity as a flagged source record is not a rendered-building pass.

The final full audit found two further baseline omissions, documented in the [supplementary rejection register](reports/additional-source-rejections.json) with [versioned GeoJSON evidence](sources/additional-building-rejections.geojson). This supplement preserves the original geometry report and four-building register hashes; it changes no runtime geometry. Both instances remain **unaccepted** source/site fidelity:

- `way/121553129`, unnamed industrial building, tile `5_-9`, model X/Z **5096.807 / -8670.319 m**. Its cooked foundation relief is **5.011039 m**, exceeding the **4.95 m** limit from 90% of its **5.5 m** model body height; its sampled buried-wall fraction is **45.2582%**, below the required 50%. The supplied 5.5 m height is a style estimate, and live OSM version 2 has no height/floor-level tags. Reference-check the actual building and stepped site/floor arrangement, then author a bounded supported foundation; do not relax limits to force a rendered count.
- `way/32617395`, **Masshouse**, tile `7_-8`, model X/Z **7560.595 / -7052.005 m**. The proposed foundation intersects secondary road **Masshouse Lane / B4114 (`way/4823750`)**, whose model road levels are approximately 114.491–114.655 m ODN; the proposed floor is 119.014 m ODN. Its buried-wall fraction is **46.8735%**, also below 50%. Live source version 14 supplies a 50 m/15-level apartment tower; road version 18 supplies three lanes and one-way operation but no tunnel/passage or surveyed clearance. Reconcile road alignment/width, floor levels, overhang/supports and any real void using current reference evidence before constructing the site. A solid plinth or invented passage is not an accepted remedy.

The full report separately lists **187 rejected building-instance records**, **843 unsupported cable-attachment candidates**, and **574 unmatched same-level road-surface probes**. The six named baseline omissions are a subset of those source/fidelity limitations. Their flags and preserved identities are not rendered-asset or whole-scene contact passes.

The dataset's geographic extraction checks are separate from game acceptance. Local recognition, exact clearance surveys, full operational infrastructure fidelity, current timetable fidelity, device behaviour, end-to-end driving, two-hour stability and RTX 3060 performance require their own actual tests. `data-validation.json` does not certify any of those gates. Preserve the embedded OpenStreetMap ODbL and Environment Agency OGL attribution.
