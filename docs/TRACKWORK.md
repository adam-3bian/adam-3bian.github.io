# Connected trackwork assets

`deriveTrackwork(route)` reconstructs simple switches from actual degree-three nodes and incident edges in the supplied eastern source graph. It identifies the common stem and two outgoing legs, chooses the outgoing leg that follows the runtime running path, and follows only degree-two continuation for the reverse branch. It never joins tracks merely because their plan coordinates are close.

The normal route is fitted directly to the shared runtime running path; the reverse closure blends the small source-fit adjustment out within twenty metres and ends exactly on the connected mapped graph edge. Definitions preserve source node/way/edge IDs, all followed branch edges, normal/reverse edge pairs and the measured fit adjustment. Source-edge identity establishes connectivity, not actual operating permission or an interlocking proof. Normal does not necessarily mean geometrically straight.

`makeSwitch(definition)` returns geometry in **world coordinates**, with one batched static mesh and two moving blade meshes. It includes profiled stock/branch rails, tapered hinged blades, closure/crossing detail, frog, wing/check rails, long bearers, a point machine and optional ballast. `setSwitchBlend(group, value)` updates the blade gaps and reports normal/reverse/moving proof plus the selected source edge pair. Components do not share mutable state.

Definitions retain the runtime unit 3D gauge direction for each normal-path sample, oriented to the switch's facing direction. `switchRailPosition(definition, 'normal' | 'branch', pathArc, offset)` interpolates and normalizes that frame before applying a gauge offset. Heads are 75 mm wide with centres at ±755 mm, leaving a physical 1,435 mm between inside faces in the canted 3D plane. Frog separation uses the 1,510 mm centre spacing. Head corner ribbons, check rails and moving blades share the canted datum; branch frames blend from the runtime toe to the connected source geometry. Batched fixed geometry and both moving blades have stable component IDs. The endpoint test checks actual rendered head-corner vertices against `RouteGeometry.railPosition` within ten millimetres, including nonzero cant and both facing directions.

Frames also retain `unitUp`, independently of facing direction. Profile and blade depth follows this orthogonal section axis; fixed rails, frogs and check rails use the same head/web/foot joints at 0/−45/−115/−140 mm. Bearers use the complete orthonormal basis, with tops at section depth 140 mm and bottoms at 290 mm. Default ballast fills the entire normal-to-branch bearer envelope rather than separate strips. `formationSections` exposes top-left/right corners at depth 290 mm and sloping lower corners at depth 640 mm for terrain support. Terrain generation must support these full quads, including low canted edges and the area between the two paths, and must not overlay another upper bed within switch spans. Rendered profile corners are tested at every normal endpoint, including undersides; rendered blade depth and full ballast envelope are checked separately.

Integration must replace the corresponding base running-rail geometry over `startS..endS` for the definition's logical lane, rather than overlaying duplicate rails. `supportPaths` supplies the complete normal/branch formation paths for ground constraints. The factory supplies the full switch ballast bed by default; only pass `{includeBallast: false}` when an external engineered bed already supports its complete `formationSections` envelope. Do not translate/rotate the returned group again.

The derivation deliberately rejects insufficiently aligned nodes, unresolved tight/compound junctions, short branches and overlapping simple-switch envelopes. The returned `gaps` records these cases and the missing western source-node graph. It does not replace unsupported western junctions with invented station-anchor points or claim that a source-connected reverse edge has current railway movement authority.
